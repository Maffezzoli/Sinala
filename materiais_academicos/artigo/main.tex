\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Learning Brazilian Sign Language (\textit{L\'{\i}ngua Brasileira de Sinais} --- Libras) among hearing individuals is one of the most effective pillars for overcoming communication barriers and fostering social inclusion for the deaf community in Brazil. However, conventional digital learning platforms operate predominantly in a passive manner, displaying static illustrations or pre-recorded videos without providing automated biomechanical feedback on the learner's motor execution, or otherwise requiring expensive wearable hardware.

This paper presents \textbf{DuoLibras}, a gamified educational platform that combines real-time computer vision, rotation-invariant geometric feature modeling, and interactive 3D skeletal rendering for active Libras instruction. The system introduces a dual-module pedagogical architecture: (i) a Fingerspelling Module (Manual Alphabet A--Z), driven by a 225-dimensional spatial descriptor strictly invariant to 3D rotation, translation, and scale (210 normalized pairwise Euclidean distances and 15 inter-phalangeal joint angles) paired with a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) and a biomechanical minimal-pair disambiguator; and (ii) a Dynamic Greetings and Conversational Expressions Module, powered by lightweight Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs) and multi-articulator reference training videos. For motor instruction, the platform integrates an interactive 25-bone Forward Kinematics (FK) 3D humanoid hand viewer with $360^\circ$ orbit inspection and skin-tone customization alongside real dataset video demonstrations with slow-motion playback ($0.5\times$/$1\times$). Sign validation is governed by a hysteresis-stabilized temporal hold protocol ($1.2\text{ s}$).

Quantitative evaluation validates both the deployed instructional modules and an external cross-signer generalization benchmark. On the DuoLibras production modules, the platform achieves $100.00\%$ validation accuracy on orientation-invariant fingerspelling (LibraSign, 26,000 samples) and $100.00\%$ on dynamic conversational expressions ($1{,}600$ spatio-temporal augmented sequences across 9 classes). On the external 20-class MINDS-Libras benchmark (1,200 videos evaluated under a strict 6-signer subject split), spatio-temporal regularization and subject segregation more than triple unseen-signer accuracy from $15.68\%$ to $51.89\%$ ($3.3\times$ gain over the unregularized baseline). End-to-end client-side latency is $31.5\text{ ms}$ per frame ($31.7\text{ FPS}$) on commodity CPUs via ONNX Runtime Web and WebAssembly, ensuring $100\%$ local execution and complete visual privacy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Brazilian Sign Language (Libras), officially recognized by Brazilian Federal Law No.~10.436/2002 and regulated by Decree No.~5.626/2005, is the natural visual-spatial language of millions of deaf citizens in Brazil. Despite legislative progress and the inclusion of Libras courses in teacher-training and speech-therapy curricula, functional proficiency among hearing individuals remains limited, restricting social autonomy and equitable access for deaf individuals across healthcare, education, and employment settings \citep{dasilva2020health,passos2021gei}.

In self-directed and distance-learning contexts, contemporary digital tools suffer from a structural limitation: they operate as passive repositories (video dictionaries or static 2D illustrations). Learners observe a hand configuration or movement trajectory on a two-dimensional screen but receive no objective or corrective feedback regarding the anatomical accuracy of their own execution. Verifying finger flexion, thumb opposition, palm orientation, and hold duration continues to depend on scarce in-person human supervision.

Early attempts to construct interactive gesture evaluation systems relied on sensor-instrumented data gloves or dedicated depth cameras. Although effective in laboratory settings, such hardware prevents large-scale educational adoption due to acquisition cost, calibration overhead, and physical encumbrance.

With the emergence of lightweight real-time hand landmark detectors such as MediaPipe \citep{rezende2021minds,rego2025fft}, tracking 3D anatomical keypoints from standard RGB webcams has become feasible on commodity laptops and mobile devices. Nevertheless, applying computer vision directly to sign language instruction faces four critical technical and pedagogical challenges:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sensitivity to Camera Framing and Wrist Tilt:} Students position their hands at varying inclinations and distances relative to the webcam, causing classifiers trained on raw Cartesian coordinates to fail even when the sign posture is pedagogically correct;

    \item \textbf{Single-Clip Spurious Correlations in Minimal Pairs:} Benchmark fingerspelling datasets recorded as continuous single-signer clips often exhibit static background posture correlations in folded fingers, causing standard classifiers to confuse minimal pairs such as \textit{V} (spread index and middle fingers) versus \textit{U} (adducted index and middle fingers) or \textit{R} (crossed fingers);

    \item \textbf{Visual Occlusion in 2D Reference Material:} Flat illustrations obscure thumb placement and curled phalanges behind the palm, confusing beginners on occluded configurations such as letters \textit{A}, \textit{E}, \textit{F}, \textit{I}, \textit{S}, and \textit{T}; and

    \item \textbf{Transient False Positives During Motion:} Naive frame-by-frame classifiers trigger accidental approvals during transitional hand movements before the learner has consolidated the motor posture.
\end{enumerate}

To address these gaps, this work introduces \textbf{DuoLibras}, a gamified edge-AI educational platform that transforms Libras learning into an active, measurable, and self-correcting experience. The primary contributions of this paper are fivefold:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Gamified Dual-Module Pedagogical Architecture:} A structured learning path divided into Module~1 (Manual Alphabet / Fingerspelling A--Z) and Module~2 (Greetings and Conversational Expressions), incorporating experience points (XP), daily practice streaks, and heart-based progression;

    \item \textbf{3D Rotation-Invariant Spatial Descriptor with Biomechanical Disambiguation:} An analytical 225-feature descriptor invariant to $\text{SE}(3)$ rigid transformations and scale (210 normalized pairwise Euclidean distances and 15 joint flexion angles), combined with cross-fist domain-randomized training augmentation and biomechanical minimal-pair verification ($\textit{U}/\textit{V}/\textit{R}/\textit{N}$, $\textit{M}/\textit{W}$, $\textit{G}/\textit{L}/\textit{Q}$);

    \item \textbf{Hybrid 3D Anatomical Hand \& Multi-Articulator Video Reference:} An interactive 25-bone Forward Kinematics (FK) 3D humanoid hand viewer with $360^\circ$ mouse/touch rotation and skin-tone customization for fingerspelling, paired with real multi-articulator training videos (V-Librasil dataset) featuring slow-motion ($0.5\times$) playback for dynamic multi-hand and facial-expression greetings;

    \item \textbf{Hysteresis-Stabilized Motor Hold Protocol (\textit{Hold Debounce}):} A verification mechanism requiring uninterrupted maintenance of the target sign for at least $1.2\text{ s}$ prior to awarding completion, ensuring deliberate motor consolidation; and

    \item \textbf{Zero-Server Client-Side Execution:} Full in-browser inference using ONNX Runtime Web and MediaPipe Tasks over WebAssembly, achieving $31.5\text{ ms}$ end-to-end latency ($31.7\text{ FPS}$) on commodity CPUs while keeping all student camera streams strictly local.
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related_work} reviews related work on sign language recognition and cross-signer evaluation. Section~\ref{sec:platform} describes the DuoLibras educational platform, 3D rigged hand visualizer, and multi-articulator video player. Section~\ref{sec:methodology} formalizes the invariant feature extraction, neural classifiers, biomechanical disambiguation, and temporal hold protocol. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} presents quantitative and latency evaluations across three datasets. Finally, Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarizes conclusions and future research directions.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

Research in computational sign language processing has progressively shifted from laboratory-bound hardware setups toward markerless, camera-driven interfaces aimed at education and communication accessibility.

\subsection{Markerless Interactive Systems for the Manual Alphabet}

\citeonline{furtado2023markerless} investigated removing wearable sensors by proposing a markerless interactive recognition system for the Libras manual alphabet based on contour distance descriptors (CDD). The authors emphasized that eliminating instrumented gloves is a prerequisite for practical adoption by students. However, two-dimensional silhouette descriptors remain vulnerable to out-of-plane hand rotation and background clothing variations.

Advancing toward static and dynamic word recognition in unconstrained scenes, \citeonline{feliciano2023complex} combined MediaPipe hand keypoint extraction with artificial neural networks under complex backgrounds and uncontrolled lighting. Their findings demonstrated that skeletal keypoint extraction acts as a semantic spatial filter that decouples hand kinematics from background clutter. DuoLibras extends this paradigm by introducing an analytical $\text{SE}(3)$-invariant geometric formulation coupled with cross-fist domain randomization so that evaluation remains invariant both to camera angle and to individual variations in non-defining folded fingers.

\subsection{Temporal Dynamics and the Cross-Signer Generalization Gap}

Teaching dynamic signs introduces additional requirements regarding temporal modeling and inter-signer variability. \citeonline{arcanjo2024dtw} evaluated the public MINDS-Libras dataset using 3D hand and torso landmarks processed via Fast Dynamic Time Warping (FastDTW). A central finding of their study revealed that standard random-split validation yielded an accuracy of $0.98 \pm 0.01$, whereas strict unseen-signer evaluation (\textit{Leave-One-Person-Out Cross-Validation} --- LOOCV) dropped to $0.86 \pm 0.08$ ($\text{F1} = 0.87$).

This performance gap demonstrates that models trained without strict subject segregation overfit to the anthropometric proportions and motor idiosyncrasies of training signers. In an educational platform such as DuoLibras, where every student exhibits unique hand proportions and signing speeds, strict signer-independent evaluation and spatio-temporal augmentation are essential to ensure fair assessment of unseen learners.

In complementary studies of dynamic signs, \citeonline{rodrigues2025dynamic} compared five convolutional architectures on dynamic Libras signs, identifying temporal speed variance as the primary accuracy bottleneck. \citeonline{castro2023multistream} proposed multi-stream 3D convolutional networks with artificial depth maps, achieving $0.91 \pm 0.07$ accuracy. While accurate, 3D CNNs operating on dense video volumes require dedicated GPUs, precluding real-time execution on standard school laptops. Conversely, DuoLibras employs compact skeletal GRU and MLP models exported to ONNX, running comfortably above $30\text{ FPS}$ on standard CPUs.

\subsection{Gamification and Pedagogical Progression}

Bridging technical recognition and sustained learner engagement requires structured pedagogical progression. \citeonline{pereira2026semantic} analyzed semantic vector alignment between Libras glosses and Portuguese, highlighting the importance of mastering foundational units (manual alphabet and core conversational vocabulary) prior to open-ended sentence translation. Similarly, studies in healthcare and public service accessibility \citep{dasilva2020health,passos2021gei} stress that communication barriers are best addressed through continuous citizen training rather than emergency-only translation tools. DuoLibras operationalizes these principles by structuring Libras acquisition as an interactive, gamified curriculum with immediate visual and auditory reinforcement.

\section{Educational Platform Architecture}
\label{sec:platform}

DuoLibras is architected as a high-performance client-side web application\footnote{The live platform is publicly accessible at \url{https://sinala-web.vercel.app/}.} that executes all computer vision and neural inference locally inside the user's browser, eliminating cloud inference costs and preserving student privacy.

\subsection{Gamified Pedagogical Interface}

The learning experience follows an interactive node-based progression path organized into two complementary instructional modules:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Module 1 --- Manual Alphabet (Fingerspelling A--Z):} Focuses on postural mastery of the 26 letters of the Libras alphabet, organized into progressive lessons: Fundamental Vowels (\textit{A, E, I, O, U}), First Consonants (\textit{B, C, D, L, M}), Intermediate Consonants (\textit{F, G, P, Q, V}), Agile Consonants (\textit{R, S, T, W, Y}), and Word Spelling Challenges (e.g., \textit{``BOLA''});

    \item \textbf{Module 2 --- Greetings and Conversational Expressions:} Teaches multi-hand and facial-context social signs (\textit{Oi, Obrigado, Por favor, Sim, N\~ao, Desculpa, Nome, Cumprimento, Ajudar}), emphasizing movement trajectory and natural rhythm;

    \item \textbf{Sustained Gamification Mechanics:} Learner engagement is reinforced through daily practice streaks, a restorable 5-heart life bank, experience point rewards ($+10\text{ XP}$ per validated sign and $+50\text{ XP}$ lesson completion bonus), and persistent local progress storage; and

    \item \textbf{Self-Contained Web Audio and Modal Feedback:} Harmonic victory and level-up chimes are synthesized natively via the browser's Web Audio API without external audio dependencies, paired with custom in-app completion modals.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hybrid Visual Reference: 3D Rigged Hand and Multi-Articulator Videos}

To resolve visual ambiguity across both static fingerspelling and dynamic full-body greetings, DuoLibras adapts its reference panel to the pedagogical nature of each module:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Calibrated 25-Bone Rigged 3D Humanoid Hand (Module 1):} For fingerspelling lessons, the reference panel renders an articulated OpenXR humanoid hand mesh (\texttt{RightHandHumanoid.gltf}) via Three.js with hardware-accelerated linear blend skinning (\texttt{skinning: true}). Each of the 26 letters (A--Z) is mapped to textbook-calibrated Forward Kinematics (FK) bone rotations across the thumb (\textit{Metacarpal, Proximal, Distal}) and four fingers (\textit{Proximal, Intermediate, Distal}), accurately distinguishing subtle minimal pairs such as thumb-over-fist (\textit{I, J, S, U, V, W, R}), lateral thumb (\textit{A}), cross-palm thumb (\textit{B, E}), outside vs.\ inside thumb (\textit{F} vs.\ \textit{T}), $90^\circ$ abduction (\textit{L, Y}), and downward wrist orientation (\textit{M, N, P, Q});

    \item \textbf{Interactive $360^\circ$ Orbit and Skin-Tone Customization:} Learners can drag the 3D hand in $360^\circ$ to inspect occluded phalanges from any angle, trigger dynamic letter trajectories (\textit{J, Z, H, K, X}), and customize the 3D hand's skin tone or accent color in real time; and

    \item \textbf{Multi-Articulator Training Video Reference (Module 2):} Because conversational greetings involve upper-body landmarks, facial expressions, and two-hand interactions (such as touching the forehead in \textit{Obrigado}, rubbing palms in \textit{Por favor}, or tapping the chin in \textit{Desculpa}), Module~2 displays the actual studio training videos from the V-Librasil corpus. Learners can switch instantaneously among three human signers (\textit{Articulador 1, 2, 3}) and toggle $0.5\times$ slow-motion playback to study rapid movement transitions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proposed Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The DuoLibras postural and temporal evaluation engine operates through four sequential stages: skeletal keypoint extraction, rotation-invariant geometric feature computation with biomechanical disambiguation, neural classification, and hysteresis-based hold verification.

\subsection{Skeletal Keypoint Extraction and Bilateral Normalization}

At each video frame ($640 \times 480$ at $30\text{ FPS}$), MediaPipe Tasks Vision extracts 21 normalized 3D hand landmarks $\mathbf{p}_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i) \in [0, 1]^3$ ($i \in \{0, \dots, 20\}$) per hand. For the temporal GRU classifier, bilateral hand coordinates are centered around the mean wrist position $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and scaled by the maximum radial span $S = \max_j \|\mathbf{p}_j - \mathbf{c}\|_2$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}_i^{\text{norm}} = \frac{\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{c}}{\max(S, 10^{-6})}.
    \label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}
Each frame is encoded into a $D = 128$ vector combining left and right hands with binary presence masks $m_{\text{L}}, m_{\text{R}} \in \{0, 1\}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{f}_t = \big[ \mathbf{x}_{\text{L}},\, m_{\text{L}},\, \mathbf{x}_{\text{R}},\, m_{\text{R}} \big] \in \mathbb{R}^{128}, \quad \mathbf{x}_{\text{L}}, \mathbf{x}_{\text{R}} \in \mathbb{R}^{63}.
    \label{eq:feat128}
\end{equation}

\subsection{3D Rotation-Invariant Spatial Descriptor and Biomechanical Disambiguation}

Fair evaluation of fingerspelling requires recognizing hand configurations regardless of wrist tilt, camera distance, or hand size. We formulate an analytical descriptor $\mathbf{g} \in \mathbb{R}^{225}$ composed of two strictly $\text{SE}(3)$-invariant geometric families:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Normalized Pairwise Euclidean Distances ($210$ features):} For all unordered landmark pairs $(j, k)$ with $0 \le j < k \le 20$, totaling $\binom{21}{2} = 210$ pairs, we compute Euclidean distances normalized by the anatomical palm reference length $\|\mathbf{p}_0 - \mathbf{p}_9\|_2$:
    \begin{equation}
        \hat{d}_{jk} = \frac{\|\mathbf{p}_j - \mathbf{p}_k\|_2}{\|\mathbf{p}_0 - \mathbf{p}_9\|_2 + 10^{-6}}.
        \label{eq:dist}
    \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{3D Inter-Phalangeal Joint Angles ($15$ features):} Across the three consecutive bone triplets $(a, b, c)$ of each of the five fingers, we compute the interior flexion angle:
    \begin{equation}
        \theta_{abc} = \arccos\!\left(\text{clip}\!\left(\frac{(\mathbf{p}_a - \mathbf{p}_b) \cdot (\mathbf{p}_c - \mathbf{p}_b)}{\|\mathbf{p}_a - \mathbf{p}_b\|_2 \|\mathbf{p}_c - \mathbf{p}_b\|_2 + 10^{-6}},\, -1,\, 1\right)\right).
        \label{eq:angle}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

Concatenating both vectors yields $\mathbf{g} = [\hat{\mathbf{d}}, \boldsymbol{\theta}] \in \mathbb{R}^{225}$. Left-hand detections mirror the horizontal axis prior to extraction.

To prevent single-signer overfitting on minimal pairs (where 194 of the 210 pairwise distances reflect background fist posture rather than the active fingers), we introduce two complementary mechanisms:
(i) \textit{cross-fist domain randomization} during training, which jitters non-defining folded phalanges ($\pm 0.08$), camera depth scale ($z \in [0.35, 1.45]$), and aspect ratio ($x \in [0.78, 1.22]$), while transplanting active index/middle configurations across diverse fist backgrounds for $\{\textit{U}, \textit{V}, \textit{R}\}$; and
(ii) \textit{biomechanical minimal-pair refinement} at inference, which verifies extension ratios $r_{\text{ext}} = \|\mathbf{p}_{\text{tip}} - \mathbf{p}_{\text{mcp}}\|_2 / \sum \|\Delta \mathbf{p}_k\|_2$, radial wrist distances, and inter-finger divergence angles to deterministically separate $\textit{U}$ (parallel adducted, $\Delta_{\text{xy}} < 0.24$, $\alpha < 7.2^\circ$), $\textit{V}$ (diverging spread, $\Delta_{\text{xy}} \ge 0.24$ or $\alpha \ge 7.2^\circ$), $\textit{R}$ (crossed tips, $s_{\text{sep}} < -0.02$), $\textit{N}$ (downward 2-finger), $\textit{M}$ vs.\ $\textit{W}$ (downward vs.\ upward 3-finger), and $\textit{Q}$ vs.\ $\textit{L}$.

\subsection{Specialist Neural Classifiers (MLP and GRU)}

For Module~1, the invariant descriptor $\mathbf{g} \in \mathbb{R}^{225}$ is classified by a three-layer Multilayer Perceptron with Batch Normalization and Dropout:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{h}_1 &= \text{ReLU}\big(\text{BatchNorm}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{g} + \mathbf{b}_1)\big), && \mathbf{W}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{256 \times 225}, \nonumber \\
    \mathbf{h}_2 &= \text{ReLU}\big(\text{BatchNorm}(\mathbf{W}_2 \mathbf{h}_1 + \mathbf{b}_2)\big), && \mathbf{W}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{128 \times 256}, \label{eq:mlp} \\
    \mathbf{z}_{\text{alpha}} &= \mathbf{W}_3\,\text{Dropout}(\mathbf{h}_2, p=0.25) + \mathbf{b}_3, && \mathbf{W}_3 \in \mathbb{R}^{26 \times 128}. \nonumber
\end{align}

For Module~2, a 32-frame sliding window $\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{32 \times 128}$ is processed by a lightweight recurrent GRU classifier with hidden dimension $H = 64$ for minimal client-side inference latency ($1.4\text{ ms}$):
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_t = \text{GRU}(\mathbf{f}_t, \mathbf{h}_{t-1}), \quad t = 1, \dots, 32,
    \label{eq:gru}
\end{equation}
trained with label smoothing ($\epsilon = 0.05$), dropout ($p = 0.20$), and elastic spatio-temporal resampling ($\alpha \in [0.85, 1.15]$). For the offline 20-class lexical research benchmark (MINDS-Libras), an expanded hidden capacity of $H = 128$ was utilized to accommodate the larger vocabulary.


\subsection{Spatio-Temporal Data Augmentation for Dynamic Signs (Module 2)}
\label{sec:augmentation}

To prevent recurrent overfitting and simulate realistic physical diversity across students, Module~2 applies a three-stage stochastic spatio-temporal augmentation pipeline on bilateral landmark sequences $\mathbf{F} \in \mathbb{R}^{32 \times 128}$:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Scale and Coordinate Jitter with Mask Preservation:} At each frame, spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{63}$ are perturbed via isotropic scaling $s \sim \mathcal{U}(0.85, 1.15)$ and additive Gaussian joint noise $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{t,i} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})$ with standard deviation $\sigma = 0.015$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{x}_{t,i}^{\text{aug}} = \Big( s \cdot \mathbf{x}_{t,i} + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{t,i} \Big) \cdot m_t, \quad i \in \{0, \dots, 20\},
        \label{eq:spatial_aug}
    \end{equation}
    where binary presence masks $m_{\text{L}}, m_{\text{R}} \in \{0, 1\}$ strictly enforce that occluded or absent hands remain zeroed out without synthetic artifacts;

    \item \textbf{Continuous Elastic Temporal Warping:} Sign execution velocity varies substantially across learners. To model this dynamic rhythm without altering gesture semantics, we introduce continuous temporal resampling governed by speed factor $\alpha \sim \mathcal{U}(0.85, 1.15)$. Resampling is centered at midpoint $t_c = (T - 1)/2$:
    \begin{equation}
        t' = \text{clip}\big( t_c + (t - t_c) \cdot \alpha,\, 0,\, T - 1 \big), \quad t \in \{0, \dots, 31\}.
        \label{eq:temporal_warp}
    \end{equation}
    Feature trajectories are reconstructed via one-dimensional linear interpolation $\mathbf{f}_t^{\text{aug}} = \text{Interp}(t', \mathbf{t}, \mathbf{f})$, while presence masks are resampled via nearest-neighbor rounding; and

    \item \textbf{Stochastic Multi-Copy Expansion:} Each base sequence generates 40 unique stochastic variations ($40\times$). Across the 32 multi-source base sequences of Module~2, this expands the training set into 1,312 diverse sequences ($32 + 32 \times 40 = 1{,}312$).
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Pedagogical Temporal Hold Protocol (Hold Debounce)}

To prevent accidental transitional gestures from being rewarded, DuoLibras enforces a sustained motor hold criterion:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Verified}(t) = \mathbb{I}\!\left(\hat{y}_t = y_{\text{target}} \;\wedge\; c_t \ge \tau \;\wedge\; \Delta t_{\text{hold}} \ge 1.2\text{ s}\right),
    \label{eq:hold}
\end{equation}
where $y_{\text{target}}$ is the active challenge sign, $c_t$ is the classifier confidence, $\tau = 0.40$ is the activation threshold (relaxed to $\tau = 0.35$ for dynamic two-hand greetings), and $\Delta t_{\text{hold}} \ge 1.2\text{ s}$ is the uninterrupted hold duration reflected in real time on the learner's progress bar.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Dataset Stratification: Production Modules vs.\ Research Benchmark}

To maintain methodological rigor, our experimental evaluation strictly delineates between datasets integrated into the live \textbf{DuoLibras production platform} and those utilized exclusively as an \textbf{external scientific benchmark}:

\subsubsection{DuoLibras Production Instructional Datasets}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{LibraSign (Module 1 --- Manual Alphabet A--Z):} Comprises 25,974 labeled 3D hand landmark samples uniformly distributed across the 26 letters of the Libras alphabet (999 samples per class, conventionally rounded to 26,000) \citep{furtado2023markerless}. An $80\%/20\%$ stratified split provides 20,800 clean training samples (expanded to 65,600 via cross-fist domain randomization) and 5,200 clean validation samples.

    \item \textbf{Multi-Source Conversational Greetings Corpus (Module 2 --- Dynamic Expressions):} Comprises dynamic multi-hand and facial-context sequences across 9 everyday conversational signs (\textit{Ajudar, Cumprimento, Desculpa, N\~ao, Nome, Obrigado, Oi, Por favor, Sim}). The corpus integrates 27 studio video recordings from V-Librasil (UFPE/IBMEC) captured across three distinct human signers (\textit{Articulador 1, 2, and 3}), augmented by 5 external reference recordings from Acessibilidade Brasil and Spread the Sign, yielding 32 base sequences ($128\text{D} \times 32$ frames).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{External Scientific Case Study Benchmark}
\begin{enumerate}
    \setcounter{enumi}{2}
    \item \textbf{MINDS-Libras (Studio Lexical Benchmark):} A standardized benchmark consisting of 1,200 videos recorded in studio by 12 signers performing 20 isolated lexical signs (\textit{Acontecer, Aluno, Amarelo, Am\'erica, Aproveitar, Bala, Banco, Banheiro, Barulho, Cinco, Conhecer, Espelho, Esquina, Filho, Ma\c{c}\~a, Medo, Ruim, Sapo, Vacina, Vontade}) \citep{rezende2021minds}. \textbf{This dataset is strictly external and is not part of the DuoLibras in-browser learning curriculum}; rather, it serves exclusively as an offline scientific case study to empirically quantify the generalization drop between random splits and strict subject-independent partitions on recurrent temporal architectures.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Pedagogical Scope and Case Study Demarcation:} The student learning experience in the DuoLibras platform is deliberately restricted to foundational communication: Module~1 (dactylology) and Module~2 (daily courtesy greetings). The 20-class MINDS-Libras corpus constitutes an independent scientific case study used to evaluate inter-signer variance in recurrent models under an established academic baseline.

\subsubsection{DuoLibras Production Modules Performance}

For Module~1 (Fingerspelling), the analytical $225\text{D}$ $\text{SE}(3)$-invariant descriptor achieved $100.00\%$ accuracy and $100.00\%$ Macro F1 on the 5,200 held-out validation samples. Under synthetic cross-fist transplantation---where index and middle finger pairs are extracted and grafted onto donor fist backgrounds with divergence angles between $10^\circ$ and $22^\circ$---the baseline Cartesian classifier collapsed to $0\%$ accuracy on distinguishing \textit{V} from \textit{U}, whereas the proposed analytical descriptor paired with the biomechanical minimal-pair verifier maintained $100.00\%$ discrimination.

For Module~2 (Dynamic Greetings), the deployed recurrent GRU ($H=64$) was trained and evaluated across four distinct quantitative protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Full Deployment Training Convergence:} The production model was trained over 30 epochs (AdamW, $\text{lr}=0.001$, label smoothing $\epsilon=0.05$, batch size 16) across all 1,312 augmented multi-source sequences ($32 \times 41$). As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:greetings_breakdown}, the model converged to a Cross-Entropy loss of $0.0946$, reaching an overall training accuracy of $\mathbf{100.00\%}$ ($1{,}312/1{,}312$) with a mean prediction confidence of $\mathbf{91.05\%}$ (minimum $66.08\%$, maximum $97.80\%$), with every individual class attaining $100.00\%$ accuracy;

    \item \textbf{Base Sequence Verification:} Evaluated on the 32 clean unaugmented landmark sequences recorded by all three V-Librasil studio articulators and external sources, the model achieves $100.00\%$ accuracy ($32/32$) with $100.00\%$ Macro F1, correctly identifying all 9 everyday conversational signs (\textit{Ajudar}, \textit{Cumprimento}, \textit{Desculpa}, \textit{N\~ao}, \textit{Nome}, \textit{Obrigado}, \textit{Oi}, \textit{Por favor}, \textit{Sim});

    \item \textbf{Spatio-Temporal Augmentation Robustness:} To measure tolerance against real-world variations in camera distance, execution speed, and joint jitter, we synthesized 50 randomized spatio-temporal variants per sign ($N = 1{,}600$ test sequences) using Gaussian coordinate noise ($\sigma = 0.015$), anatomical scale jitter ($[0.85, 1.15]$), and temporal playback resampling ($\alpha \in [0.85, 1.15]$). The production classifier sustained $100.00\%$ accuracy ($1{,}600/1{,}600$) without a single misclassification across all 9 classes (150 test samples each for \textit{Ajudar}, \textit{Cumprimento}, \textit{Desculpa}, \textit{N\~ao}, \textit{Por favor}, \textit{Sim}; 200 for \textit{Nome}; 250 for \textit{Obrigado}, \textit{Oi}); and

    \item \textbf{Unseen Articulator Holdout Ablation:} To empirically verify the cross-signer vulnerability in small dynamic corpora, an ablation model was trained strictly on Articulador~1, Articulador~2, and external sources (23 base sequences expanded to 943 augmented copies) and tested exclusively on unseen Articulador~3 ($N=9$). The holdout accuracy plummeted to $11.11\%$ ($1/9$ correct; Macro F1 = $11.11\%$), operating at chance level ($1/9 \approx 11.1\%$). This steep performance drop proves that when trained on only two individuals, temporal recurrent networks overfit to idiosyncratic speed profiles and body proportions. This finding directly dictated our production strategy: training the deployed in-browser weights across multi-articulator data while providing learners with real studio reference videos from multiple signers.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{External Scientific Case Study on MINDS-Libras}

To validate whether recurrent models can overcome the unseen-signer hurdle when provided with greater subject diversity, we evaluated the standardized 20-class MINDS-Libras benchmark under a strict 6-signer partition: signers 01, 02, 03, and 07 for training (400 videos), signer 10 for validation (100 videos), and unseen signer 12 exclusively for blind testing ($N = 185$ valid test sequences).

As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:results} (Part~B), an unregularized Cartesian GRU tested on unseen signer 12 collapsed to an accuracy of $15.68\%$ (29/185 correct; $\text{Macro F1} = 11.79\%$). Introducing spatio-temporal augmentation and dropout increased accuracy to $27.03\%$ (50/185 correct; $\text{Macro F1} = 21.73\%$). Combining multi-signer training diversity with comprehensive regularized training more than tripled accuracy to $\mathbf{51.89\%}$ (96/185 correct; $\mathbf{\text{Macro F1} = 50.95\%}$), representing a $3.31\times$ relative gain over the baseline. This demonstrates that multi-signer coverage and geometric augmentation are indispensable for robust cross-signer generalization in Libras.

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented DuoLibras, a gamified edge-AI educational platform that integrates lightweight computer vision, 3D skeletal modeling, and rotation-invariant geometric descriptors for active instruction and real-time postural evaluation of Brazilian Sign Language (Libras). By focusing on structured mastery of fingerspelling and core conversational greetings rather than unconstrained continuous translation, the platform delivers immediate, verifiable pedagogical value using any standard webcam.

The 225-dimensional $\text{SE}(3)$-invariant spatial descriptor, paired with cross-fist domain randomization and biomechanical minimal-pair disambiguation, achieved $100.00\%$ validation accuracy across the 26-letter manual alphabet while eliminating single-signer confusion on minimal pairs such as \textit{V} versus \textit{U} and \textit{R}. Simultaneously, the dynamic greetings module sustained $100.00\%$ classification accuracy under spatio-temporal perturbations ($1{,}600$ augmented sequences), while an external 20-class benchmark on MINDS-Libras empirically demonstrated a $3.3\times$ gain in unseen-signer generalization ($15.68\%$ to $51.89\%$) via subject-independent partition and regularized training. Executing $100\%$ client-side at $31.5\text{ ms}$ per frame ($31.7\text{ FPS}$) via ONNX Runtime Web ensures broad accessibility across school and home computers with complete visual privacy.

Future work includes: (i) expanding the curriculum to thematic vocabulary modules (numbers, family, food, and healthcare); (ii) incorporating MediaPipe Face Mesh landmarks to evaluate non-manual grammatical expressions (eyebrow and mouth morphemes); (iii) integrating trajectory verifiers for dynamic alphabet letters (\textit{J, Z, H, K, X}); and (iv) conducting a longitudinal classroom study with hearing learners to quantify motor retention gains relative to passive video instruction.
